## Reading each one

**H1 splits.** The half the pitch rests on holds and the half we hoped for does not.
Ranking alerts by how loud they are scores 0.483 — at chance, and
slightly *below* it, exactly as pre-registered. Our own predictor reaches
0.732, short of the 0.80 we claimed. Our target was optimistic and we
say so rather than moving the goalpost. On the operational label — does this reach
the health layer, which is what an operator acts on — the gap is much larger: 0.493 against 0.756. The pre-registered label is reported first,
because choosing the flattering label after seeing results is the exact thing
pre-registration exists to prevent.

**H2 fails at -2.6%**, and the direction is the interesting part.
The mined kernel does **not** beat a declared interdependency matrix at predicting
cascade volume once both are given the same background failure rate — it is
slightly worse (261 against 254 RMSE).

An earlier run had the mined kernel ahead by 13.8%, and that margin was ours by
construction: the declared baseline had been built with `mu = 0` while the learned
kernel carried the Hawkes background term. The background rate is a base failure
rate any operator computes by counting — it is not the contested part of H2, which
is about interdependency *structure*. Equalise it and the advantage disappears.

What survives is narrower and worth stating plainly: **mining recovers the
propagation structure that a declared matrix omits** — load redistribution between
flow-peers is 80% of the propagation mass here and appears in no declared matrix —
**but recovering it does not translate into better volume prediction.** See `h2.md`.

**H3** was pre-registered as *strictly* more damage prevented at every budget from 1
to 10. We tested one budget (5), so the claim is only partly tested.
But the prior question is sharper and we did not ask it until late: **does any arm
beat protecting five assets at random?** A random pick prevents damage too, with a
standard deviation around 176,000 on a single scenario. Against that baseline —
A1: beats chance, A2: beats chance, A3: **not distinguishable from chance**, A4: **not distinguishable from chance**.

That is the result this project did not expect and does not get to bury. Ranking
which alarm becomes a catastrophe works. Choosing *where to intervene* from that
ranking does not, and static betweenness — no learning at all — does.

**H4 is the one we wrote down expecting to lose**, and it is reported whichever way it
lands: branching ratio 0.732 against static betweenness 0.662, delta +0.070.
Against our own prediction, the kernel does beat centrality by more than the 0.05 we said it would not exceed. Being wrong in your own favour deserves more scrutiny than being wrong against it, so the ablation is where this should be judged, not here.

## Method violations, self-reported

- The H1 test seeds were scored three times while chasing two integration bugs,
  against a protocol requiring the test set be touched once. The reported number was
  regenerated on a seed range never previously scored. Disclosed rather than dropped.
- The simulator's hazard intensities were calibrated after the first ablation ran,
  because every scenario was saturating and no intervention could move the outcome.
  That is a change to the *simulator*, not to a model, and every arm was re-run
  against it. See `docs/FINDINGS.md`.
