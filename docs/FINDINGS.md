| 2026-09-09 | A | **Realism gate FAILED on first run. Two distinct causes, both real bugs, neither a tuning problem.** Reported here before fixing, per AGENTS.md rule 8. |
| 2026-09-09 | A | **Cause 1 — my power-law fitter was broken.** The x_min search scanned all unique cascade sizes and selected x_min=128, deep inside the finite-size cutoff (city has 1063 nodes, max cascade 213). It fitted the cutoff as though it were the scaling regime and returned alpha=17.7. Restricting x_min to a sensible range recovers **alpha = 1.51 at x_min=2, rising to 2.09 at x_min=20 — squarely inside the [1.3, 2.5] target.** The heavy tail was there all along; the estimator was reading the wrong part of the distribution. |
| 2026-09-09 | A | **Cause 2 — a 120-member load-redistribution clique.** KS was genuinely rejecting (0.13-0.20 against a 0.011 critical value) because the CCDF has a bump around size 50: counts fall 104 at size 20, 38 at size 30, then rise to 135 at size 50. Traced it to flow topology, not dependency topology — dependency fan-out is healthy (max 6 dependents). All 120 telecom towers had `telecom.bh_01` as their sole flow parent, making every tower a sibling of every other, so a single 120-node Motter-Lai redistribution group either barely moves or avalanches wholesale. That produces a *characteristic* cascade size, which is precisely the "threshold not a process" failure mode `docs/02-CITY-SPEC.md` warns is the subtle one. Real telecom has regional backhaul, not one national node. |
| 2026-09-09 | A | **Also observed:** 52% of cascades are size 1 — failures that propagate to nothing. That is correct and expected, and it is exactly the near-miss population Lane B will mine. 296 nodes have a single flow parent (no redundancy), which is realistic for distribution networks but worth knowing when interpreting arm A2 centrality results. |
| 2026-09-09 | A | **Cause 3, the real one — the system is SUBCRITICAL, and I nearly missed it by blaming my own gate.** After fixing the fitter and the telecom clique the gate still failed, so I tested whether the criterion itself was too strict (KS against an asymptotic critical value at n=15k rejects almost any imperfect fit — a known error that Clauset et al. exists to address). It is not too strict. A Vuong likelihood-ratio test of power-law against exponential favours **exponential at every x_min, decisively (p ~ 0)**. The tail is heavier than normal — max is 9.4 sd above the mean, P(size>=50)=0.083 — but it decays exponentially. In branching-process terms that is subcritical: rho(G) < 1, cascades die out geometrically. 52% of cascades are size 1 and the median is 1, which is consistent. A power law only emerges near criticality. Recorded before any parameter was touched. |
| 2026-09-09 | A | **Meshing the power layer moves toward criticality but does not reach it.** Real urban distribution is meshed with normally-open tie switches, so feeders were cross-connected and substations ringed. Max cascade 207 -> 333 and LR -1823 -> -1387 (less exponential), but the verdict stays exponential. Adopted anyway because it is the more realistic topology, not because of the gate. |
| 2026-09-09 | A | **DECISION: stop pushing, and report the negative result.** Further parameter movement toward a power law would be fitting the simulator to the gate rather than to reality, which is the failure AGENTS.md rule 8 exists to prevent. The power-law claim in the literature (Dobson/Carreras; restated in PI-GN-JODE) is established for **transmission-grid blackouts**. Our system is a multi-sector urban lifeline network, where that has not been established and may well be false — different mechanism, different coupling, mostly non-electrical. Asserting it would have been an unexamined transfer from one domain to another. |
| 2026-09-09 | A | **The gate was replaced with what it actually needs to establish**, and the original power-law test is retained and REPORTED AS FAILING rather than deleted. What the project actually requires of the simulator: cascade sizes span orders of magnitude (1 to 333), the tail is far heavier than thin-tailed (max is 9.4 sd above the mean, P(size>=50)=8%), it is neither all-tiny nor all-collapse (median 2, 52% size 1), and it is not bimodal (20% mid-range mass). Those are the properties that make prediction possible and discrimination measurable. Whether the tail is exactly Pareto is not one of them. |
