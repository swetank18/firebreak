# Demo runbook — six keys, nothing typed

**Owner: Lane E, with Lane F on the numbers.** Rehearse it five times. The person presenting does not touch a terminal.

Everything runs from `console/data/demo.json`, regenerated by `scripts/export_demo.py`. `scripts/build_site.py` renders it into two pages: **`www/sim/index.html`, the console the demo is driven from**, and `www/index.html`, the write-up with the evidence tables. Present from the console; the write-up is what you leave behind. **Offline. No network. No live model call.** If the wifi dies mid-pitch nothing changes.

**The numbers below are deliberately not written here.** They are fields in the exported scenario and they appear on screen; writing them into this document is how a runbook goes stale and a presenter says a number the map contradicts. Run `python scripts/export_demo.py` to print the current ones. The three runs are the same seed with the same paired randomness, so beats 3, 4 and 5 are a genuine rewind rather than three unrelated simulations.

---

## The six beats

### `1` — The city
Chennai loads. Five layers fade in: power (amber), water (blue), roads (grey), telecom (violet), hospitals (white). Rotate once, settle.

> "Five systems. Twelve hundred assets. Nobody manages them together, because nobody owns them together."

### `2` — Two alarms
Monsoon scenario starts. Two amber alerts appear side by side with **anomaly scores within 0.02 of each other** — chosen from the run by the exporter, never hand-picked. Firebreak flags one red and leaves the other amber, because their branching ratios are far apart.

> "Same alarm. Same score. Every monitoring system in this city treats these as identical. We don't."

Pause. Let them read the two identical numbers.

### `3` — Do nothing
Play. The quiet alarm stays quiet. The flagged one propagates: power → pumping → water mains → hospitals → generators drawing down → **the diesel route floods** → the generators go. The counters climb to whatever this run's do-nothing outcome is, and the hospital counter is the one to point at.

> "Above one, a cascade grows. Below one it dies. That is the whole criterion."

### `4` — Rewind and intervene
Same seed, rewound. Apply Firebreak's chosen intervention — targets, cost and **deadline** are on screen, computed by `decision/search.py`. Replay. Damage drops.

> "Not the biggest node. Not the loudest alarm. A road, because the road carried the fuel."

### `5` — The intervention that fails
Rewind again. Apply the human-obvious action: **harden the most critical assets**, same budget, five of them. Replay. **It still cascades**, and does worse than Firebreak's action.

> "This is what an experienced operator does. It is the right instinct and it does not work, because the hospital was never the problem."

**Beat 5 must fail.** If a code change ever makes beat 5 succeed, the demo is broken. `scripts/export_demo.py` refuses to write the file, and `service/tests/test_demo_beats.py::test_beat5_the_human_obvious_action_still_cascades` fails in CI. Neither asserts a 200.

### `6` — The evidence
Cut to the evidence section: the seven-arm table, the AUC bars, and the checks that failed alongside the ones that passed. Every row is read from `eval/results/*.json` at build time. The pre-registration diff is `eval/results/prereg_diff.md`, including the two predictions that went against us.

> "Ranking alerts by how loud they are predicts catastrophe about as well as a coin. We pre-registered that claim before we ran it. Here is the diff."

Stop. Do not thank them yet — take the question.

## Rules

- **The renderer computes nothing.** Every number on screen is a field in the scenario file. The console shows live counts as the clock runs and the settled person-hours totals per run; it never scales a total by elapsed time to fake a running figure.
- **Drive it from `/sim/`, not from the write-up.** The console is full-viewport and nothing scrolls, so there is no page to lose your place in on stage.
- **There is no 3D and no map tiles**, so there is nothing to fall back from: the console is a 2D canvas over the same exported schema, with no WebGL and no network. This started as the fallback and became the whole renderer, which removed a failure mode rather than mitigating it.
- **A backup video is NOT yet recorded.** Listed as outstanding rather than assumed — see `docs/LIMITATIONS.md`.
- Timer visible to the presenter only.
- No terminal on screen. No IDE. No `npm run dev` in front of judges.

## Likely questions

**Is any of this an LLM?** No. Kernel estimation is a Hawkes MLE, rollout is Monte Carlo, intervention search is enumeration and scoring. Every decision is auditable line by line. An LLM writes nothing the decision depends on.

**Is the city real?** It is geo-anchored procedural topology at Chennai's real scale and shape — not an OSM extract. Power and water asset topology is not public in India, so it is synthesised. That is stated in `docs/LIMITATIONS.md` before you ask, and the demo scenario is one seed selected from eight, which is also stated.

**Hasn't Tsinghua already done this?** I³ predicts cascade probability and volume. It selects no intervention and it trains on synthetic sweeps. It was intended as arm A5, run unmodified — **we did not reproduce it on our hardware, and the arms table says so** rather than dropping the comparison. It is our baseline, not our competitor, and right now it is a cited baseline rather than a run one.

**Where does the near-miss data come from in a real deployment?** SCADA historians already log every trip, every voltage excursion, every pump restart. Utilities keep years of it and analyse almost none of it. We need event timestamps and asset IDs — nothing else, and nothing that identifies a person.

**What if the branching ratio is wrong?** Then the deadline is wrong and we say so. Every `Criticality` carries a bootstrap CI and thin edges (`n_obs` small) widen it. We also know the direction of the error: the attribution window censors slow links, so delay is under-estimated and **deadlines read optimistic** — the system thinks its window closes sooner than it does. Safe direction, stated rather than discovered.

**Have you tested on real cascade data?** No. Real datasets underneath the layers, a simulated city on top, and honest labelling of which is which. A real utility historian feed is the ask.

## The ask — close on this, not on "thank you"

Two things. **One utility or municipal historian feed** — the near-miss data already exists and nobody is looking at it. And **a mentor who has sat in a city control room during a monsoon**, because the intervention cost model is the part we cannot get right from outside.
